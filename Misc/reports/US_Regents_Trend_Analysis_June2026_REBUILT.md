# U.S. History & Government Regents Exam
## Question-Trend Analysis and June 2026 Forecast

*Rebuilt from question-level data — supersedes prior drafts.*

---

## Executive Summary

This report analyzes the nine most recent administrations of the New York State Regents Examination in U.S. History and Government (June 2023 through January 2026) to characterize how Part I content is distributed and how it shifts from one exam to the next, and to forecast the most probable content of the June 2026 examination at both the unit level and the content-standard level.

The analysis was rebuilt from the question-by-question coding of each exam after an audit found that the summary tables in the source workbook were unreliable (see Section 1). The corrected data show a more evenly distributed exam than earlier summaries suggested: most framework units contribute between two and six Part I questions per administration, and the large single-unit "spikes" that appeared in earlier summaries were largely artifacts of mislabeled summary tabs. Three patterns nonetheless support forecasting: a small set of content standards recurs on nearly every exam; declines in a unit's question count rarely continue into the following exam; and content that anchors the essays tends to be rested in the multiple-choice section.

## 1. Data and Methodology

The analysis covers Part I (28 multiple-choice questions) of each of the nine administrations from June 2023 through January 2026. Each question was coded to its NYS framework unit (11.01–11.11) and to a specific content standard (e.g., 11.09a.2, Containment Policies), and tagged for stimulus type and for whether it requires outside information beyond the document.

**Sources and a data-integrity note.** The source workbook contains two kinds of tabs: per-question "Itemization" tabs and pre-summarized "Analysis" tabs. During this rebuild, the Analysis tabs were found to be unreliable — several are mislabeled, and their unit totals do not match the underlying questions. Verification against the released exam PDFs (e.g., the June 2024 Itemization matches the June 2024 exam question-for-question) confirmed that the **Itemization tabs are accurate and the Analysis summaries are not.** All figures in this report were therefore recomputed from the question level:

- **June 2023 – June 2025 (seven exams):** coded from the workbook Itemization tabs.
- **January 2026:** coded from the official "US May Final" data file (the May final administered to students was the January 2026 Regents).
- **August 2025:** coded by the analyst directly from the released PDF; no official itemization was available, so this column carries more uncertainty than the others.

Earlier drafts of this report inherited the Analysis-tab errors, which inflated several historical counts (for example, Industrialization in June 2023 and Civil Rights in January 2024). Those figures have been corrected throughout.

## 2. Overall Structural Trends

Five patterns recur across the nine exams and inform the June 2026 forecast.

**Trend 1 — Within each unit, questions cluster on a few standards.** A unit is not tested evenly across its sub-topics. Cold War questions are dominated by Containment (14 of the unit's questions over nine exams); the Depression era is dominated by the New Deal. Forecasting at the unit level alone misses this concentration.

**Trend 2 — A dip usually marks a floor, not a slide.** When a unit's count falls from one exam to the next, the following exam recovers 46% of the time and holds flat 46% of the time; it falls further only 8% of the time. A low reading is far more often a trough than the start of a sustained decline.

**Trend 3 — Each exam introduces some new content, but the rate has fallen as the item bank matured.** Every exam includes at least a few content standards making their first appearance. The share of first-time standards has declined steadily from 100% on the first exam studied (June 2023) to roughly 20–28% on the most recent exams; January 2026 contained 5 debut standards among 21 unique standards (24%). Of the 111 leaf-level standards in the Grade 11 framework, 71 have appeared at least once and 40 have never been tested.

**Trend 4 — The written and multiple-choice portions are complementary.** When a topic anchors an essay, it tends to be rested in the multiple-choice section. Prosperity & Depression (11.07) is the clearest case: its multiple-choice presence has fallen to one or two questions over the same period in which it has repeatedly anchored the short-essay and Civic Literacy prompts.

**Trend 5 — Most questions require outside knowledge.** About 77% of multiple-choice questions cannot be answered from the document alone; they require the student to supply outside information. This burden is unevenly distributed across content and stimulus type (Section 7).

## 5. Dips and Rebounds

To test whether low readings recover, every exam-over-exam decline in a unit's count was classified by what happened on the next administration.

| Outcome after a dip | Frequency | Share |
|---|---|---|
| Rebound (next exam higher) | 12 | 46% |
| Flat (next exam equal) | 12 | 46% |
| Deeper (next exam lower) | 2 | 8% |

A dip recovers or holds 92% of the time; a continued decline is rare. This is the empirical basis for treating most low readings as troughs.

**Behavior by unit.**
- **Reliable recoverers:** 11.05, 11.06, and 11.09 have repeatedly rebounded from low readings; 11.09 returned to 4 in January 2026.
- **The exception is 11.07 (Prosperity & Depression):** after its single spike of 8 in January 2024, it has declined or held flat for five straight exams. This pattern reflects one extended decline from one peak, not repeated failures to recover, and is best explained by Trend 4 (essay substitution) rather than by a loss of importance.
- **11.02 (Constitutional Foundations)** is the unit to watch: it held at 6 through August 2025 before dropping to 2 in January 2026. A single sharp drop from a high, stable level — its history shows it returning to 5–6 after every prior low — makes a rebound the base-rate expectation, though the unit's wide range (2–6) keeps the exact magnitude uncertain.

**Behavior by content standard.**
- *Overdue (high lifetime frequency, recently cold):* **11.03a.1 American Nationalism** — eight lifetime appearances but none in the last three exams; the most overdue high-frequency standard.
- *Ever-present (rarely absent):* **11.09a.2 Containment** — appears on eight of nine exams, including January 2026; not "due to return" so much as nearly always present.
- *Recently saturated (likely to rest):* **11.06a.2 Spanish-American War**, tested on both of the last two exams; and **11.05a.3 Regulation vs. Monopolies**, tested on three of the last five.

## 6. Debut Standards

Every exam introduces a few content standards for the first time, but the rate has declined as the item bank has matured — from 100% of standards on the first exam studied to roughly 20–28% recently. August 2025 introduced 5 debut standards; January 2026 introduced 5 (Enlightenment & British Traditions, the Nuremberg Trials, the Antiwar Movement & Watergate, Civil Rights Protests including Little Rock, and Patriot Act civil-liberties). Of the 111 leaf-level standards in the framework, 40 have never been tested.

**Predicting debuts.** Which specific standard debuts is not predictable, but its timing is constrained: 69% of debuts occur on an exam where the parent unit is at or above its own average count. A standard rarely makes its first appearance while its unit is quiet. Practically, the untested standards of an *active* forecast unit are live candidates; the untested standards of a quiet unit tend to stay dormant regardless of how long they have waited.

**Debuts rarely recur immediately.** Only about 20% of debut standards reappear on the very next exam. Reviewing the specific standards that debuted in January 2026 is therefore a low-yield use of preparation time; durable, repeatedly tested standards are the better investment.

## 8. Written Portion–Multiple-Choice Relationship

The complementarity between the essays and multiple choice (Trend 4) is the strongest caution against unit-level forecasting. Prosperity & Depression (11.07) occupied three of the six written slots across the two most recent exams. The reasonable inference is not that the Depression era is building toward a multiple-choice resurgence, but that the exam has been meeting its 11.07 coverage through the essays — which coincides with its multiple-choice count falling to one or two questions. By the same logic, recently used essay topics (Prohibition, the Erie Canal, Government Regulation of Business) are unlikely to anchor the June 2026 essays.

**Most probable June 2026 essay anchors.**
- *Short-essay sets:* fresh material from the Cold War (11.09) or the WWI era (11.06), neither of which has anchored a recent prompt.
- *Civic Literacy Essay:* the Civil Rights Movement (11.10) is the leading candidate — a recurring civic-issue topic not used recently — with Women's Suffrage and Immigration as secondary possibilities.

## 9. June 2026 Forecast

The forecast distinguishes a prediction's **magnitude** (the expected number of questions) from **confidence** in it (the probability the actual count falls in the stated range). These are independent: a low forecast can be high-confidence. The 28-question budget also applies — because the forecast ranges sum to more than 28, not every unit can land at the top of its range.

### High confidence

**11.02 Constitutional Foundations — 4–6.** The exam's only above-four unit (4.6 average). It held at 6 as recently as August 2025 before a single-exam drop to 2 in January 2026; its history shows it returning to 5–6 after every prior low. The rebound is the base-rate expectation. The range remains 4–6 rather than 5–6 because the unit's swings (2–6) are themselves a caution.

**11.09 Cold War — 3–4.** A reliable recoverer that returned to 4 in January 2026, and the home of Containment (11.09a.2), which appears on eight of nine exams. At least one containment question is the single safest standard-level prediction on the exam. The unit is also fresh for the essays, so nothing is suppressing its multiple-choice presence.

**11.08 World War II — 1–2.** A consistently light unit; one or two questions on five of the last six exams. Low and predictable.

**11.01 Colonial Foundations — 2.** The most stable unit on the exam: exactly two questions on each of the last four administrations.

### Medium-high confidence

**11.06 Rise of American Power / WWI — 3–4.** Settled at 3–4 over the last three exams. Its most-used standard, the Spanish-American War (11.06a.2), appeared on both of the last two exams and is due to rest under the two-in-a-row pattern, which is a modest downward pressure on the count.

**11.04 Reconstruction — 2–3.** A light unit that has produced two questions on most exams and three in January 2026; its variance fits comfortably in a narrow band.

**11.07 Prosperity & Depression — 1–2.** The recent record is low and stable (2, 2, 2, 2, 1), supporting a forecast below the unit's 2.8 average. The "never rebounds" pattern is thin (one extended decline from one spike), and there is genuine upside: the New Deal (11.07c.5) remains the second most-tested standard and appeared in January 2026, and the essays that have absorbed 11.07 coverage are themselves due to rotate off it. If they do, the unit could move back toward 3. The central estimate is 1–2, with the upside flagged.

### Medium confidence

**11.05 Industrialization — 3–4.** Volatile (a 1–6 range historically) but settled near four recently. Its standards (Regulation vs. Monopolies, Urbanization & Immigration) recur, but the unit's swings limit confidence in any specific count.

**11.10 Civil Rights & Social Change — 2–4.** Oscillates between two and four. It is the leading Civic Literacy Essay candidate; if it anchors the essay, its multiple-choice count may stay at the lower end rather than rise, since the exam tends not to double up.

**11.03 Expansion & Sectionalism — 2–4.** Below its earlier levels for three straight exams (2, 2, 3). The rebound case rests on American Nationalism (11.03a.1), the most overdue high-frequency standard, but the unit has not yet shown the recovery, so the range stays wide.

**11.11 U.S. in a Changing World — 1–2.** Light and erratic (absent on three of nine exams; never above two). A consistently minor unit.

### Standard-level priorities

The most dependable specific expectations for June 2026 are **Containment (11.09a.2)**, near-certain to appear; **the New Deal (11.07c.5)**, likely to appear even if its unit stays low; a **Constitutional Foundations rebound (11.02)**; and the return of **American Nationalism (11.03a.1)** if 11.03 is active. Per Trend 1, these standard-level calls are more reliable than the unit ranges that contain them.

## 10. Limitations

- Nine administrations is a modest sample; single-exam swings are large relative to the averages, and confidence levels reflect tendencies rather than guarantees.
- The August 2025 column is the analyst's reading of the released PDF, not an official itemization, and may differ at the margins; the January 2026 coding is from the official May Final file (with one analyst-applied correction to Q22 and Q27).
- Per-unit outside-information and content-standard percentages rest on small samples for the lighter units and should be read as approximate.
- Dip-and-rebound logic is probabilistic; the exam writers are not bound by it, and an unusually high or low reading can persist against the base rate. The 11.02 forecast in particular carries real uncertainty.
- Forecasts address which content is likely to appear, not how difficult individual questions will be.

---

## 11. Bottom Line and Teaching Implications

Review at the level of **content standards, not eras**, and judge predictions by reliability rather than size. The most dependable expectations for June 2026 are a mix of probable rebounds and stable lows: a Constitutional Foundations recovery (11.02) and a Cold War showing anchored by Containment (11.09a.2), alongside confident low forecasts for World War II and Colonial Foundations. Treat Prosperity & Depression (11.07) as a low-but-watch unit — the essays that have absorbed it are due to rotate off, which could lift its multiple-choice count. Because almost four in five questions require outside knowledge and even the most document-supported units demand it on two-thirds of items, content mastery — not document-reading technique alone — is what the exam rewards. Finally, spend little time on the standards that debuted in January 2026 (they rarely recur) and instead concentrate on the standards that have proven they recur, prioritizing the untested standards only of units forecast to be active.
