## 7. June 2026 Forecast and the Reasoning Behind It

The forecast separates two things the original report conflated: the **magnitude** of a prediction (a high or low question count) and the **confidence** in it (how likely the actual count lands in the stated range). These are independent. A low forecast can be a high-confidence call — predicting that World War II stays at one or two questions is one of the surest statements in this report. Accordingly, the highest-confidence units below are a deliberate mix of probable rebounds and stable lows.

A second discipline now applies throughout: **the 28-question budget.** Because the ranges collectively exceed 28, not every unit can land high. The optimistic rebound calls compete with one another for a finite number of slots, which is a structural reason to weight them toward the lower ends of their ranges.

### High-confidence calls

**11.02 Constitutional Foundations — forecast 4–6, High.** The highest-volume unit on the exam (5.1 average) sat at its normal 6 as recently as August 2025, then dropped sharply to 2 in January 2026 — a single-exam anomaly, not a sustained decline (an earlier coding error had it looking like a two-exam slide). The base-rate rebound logic, the unit's status as the backbone, and its freedom to return to multiple choice now that it last anchored an essay in August 2025 all point upward. The confidence is in the *direction and presence*; the range stays 4–6 rather than a committed 5–6 only because a one-exam plunge this steep shows the unit can swing.

**11.09 Cold War — forecast 3–5, High.** The unit rebounded to 4 in January 2026 and is a reliable rebounder. The decisive factor is its iron-man standard, Containment (11.09a.2), which has appeared in eight of the nine exams studied (14 questions lifetime, including the Marshall Plan question in January 2026). It is less "due to return" than simply ever-present: at least one containment question is the safest single-standard bet on the exam. The unit is also fresh for the essays, so nothing is suppressing its multiple-choice presence.

**11.07 Prosperity & Depression — forecast 1–2, High (the contrarian call).** This is where era-level intuition fails most clearly, and where separating magnitude from confidence matters most. A naive model sees a unit at a low and predicts a rebound; it also sees 11.07 dominating recent essays and reads that as importance. Both are wrong. 11.07 is the **only** unit that has never rebounded, and its essay dominance is the *cause* of its multiple-choice disappearance, not a precursor to its return. Having filled three of six recent essay slots, it is more likely to rest across the whole exam. The forecast value is low; the confidence in that low value is high.

**11.08 World War II — forecast 1–2, High.** A structurally light unit, range-bound at one or two questions in five of the last six administrations (the lone exception being a 6-question spike in June 2024). Low and predictable.

**11.01 Colonial Foundations — forecast 2, High.** The single most stable unit on the test: exactly two questions on each of the last four exams. The narrowest forecast in the report, and among the most confident.

### Medium-confidence calls

**11.06 Rise of American Power / WWI — forecast 3–4, Medium-High.** A reliable high-volume performer that always appears, which makes its *presence* a near-certainty. But the specific count is more constrained than the original report implied. After peaking at 8 in January 2025, it has settled to 4, 3, 4 — at or below its 4.6 average. Forecasting 4–5 would have placed the whole range above where it has actually landed. Compounding the downside, its iron-man standard, the Spanish-American War (11.06a.2), appeared in both of the last two exams and is due to rest under the same two-in-a-row logic applied elsewhere. The honest call is 3–4, with high confidence it appears and medium confidence in the exact range.

**11.04 Reconstruction — forecast 2–4, Medium-High.** The most document-friendly unit, always present at low volume. The range is wide enough to capture its normal variance, which is why confidence is reasonable; the recent rebound to 4 may settle back toward 2–3.

**11.05 Industrialization — forecast 3–5, Medium.** Genuinely volatile, with a lifetime range from 1 to 8. It anchored the August 2025 Civic Literacy Essay, so a multiple-choice surge is unlikely, but its swings make any specific range only a medium-confidence bet.

**11.10 Civil Rights & Social Change — forecast 2–4, Medium.** Oscillates between 2 and 4 in recent exams. It is the leading candidate to anchor the June 2026 Civic Literacy Essay; if it does, its multiple-choice count may stay capped rather than spike, since the exam tends not to double up on a single unit.

**11.03 Expansion & Sectionalism — forecast 2–5, Medium.** The rebound thesis is sound — the unit is below its 4.4 average and its staple standard, American Nationalism (11.03a.1), is the most overdue on the exam — but it is unproven. The unit has been stuck at 2 for two straight exams and has not yet shown the recovery, so the wide range reflects real uncertainty about whether the rebound arrives in June or later.

**11.11 U.S. in a Changing World — forecast 1–2, Medium.** Structurally light and erratic: absent (0) on three of the nine exams and never above 2 (an earlier coding error had overstated August 2025 at 3; corrected, it was 1, because the 2000-election questions belong to 11.02d.3). A consistently minor unit.

## 9. Bottom Line

**Teach to the standards, not the eras — and judge predictions by their reliability, not their size.** The most reliable expectations for June 2026 are not all "big" calls: alongside the probable rebounds of Constitutional Foundations (11.02) and the Cold War (11.09) — especially the near-certain return of Containment (11.09a.2) — sit equally confident *low* calls for World War II and Colonial Foundations. Prosperity & Depression (11.07) is the genuine wildcard: it has sat at 1–2 for five exams, but the essays that have been absorbing its coverage are themselves due to rotate off it, which could send it back toward its baseline of 3 — so treat it as a watch item, not a settled low. Because the forecast ranges collectively exceed the 28-question budget, expect the rebounds to be partial: not every high-volume unit can recover in the same sitting. Finally, give students a brief defensive pass over the never-tested canon — the Cuban Missile Crisis, Pearl Harbor, and Hoover vs. FDR — in case the examiners reach for fresh material to fill the slots the rotation leaves open.
