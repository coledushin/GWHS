Question quality difficulty vs. point-biserial

How to read point-biserial (PB)

A question is only useful if it tells students apart. PB measures that: it is the correlation between getting this question right and scoring well on the rest of the multiple-choice questions (the question itself is left out, so it can't inflate its own score). It runs from −1 to +1.

  • High PB — students who did well elsewhere got it right; students who struggled missed it.
  • Near zero — right and wrong answers are scattered. The question isn't revealing who understands.
  • Negative — stronger students missed it more than weaker ones. Usually a wrong answer key or a distractor that sounds more right than the answer.
  • Strong0.30+Cleanly separates students who know the material from those who don't.
  • Acceptable0.20–0.29Does its job; no action needed.
  • Weak0.10–0.19Only loosely tied to overall performance — check the wording and distractors.
  • Poorbelow 0.10Almost no relationship, or negative. Check the answer key first.
Top right · easy & discriminatingHealthy. Most students get it, and the few who miss it are the ones struggling.
Top left · hard & discriminatingWhere your strongest students pull ahead. Good questions, so a low % correct here isn't a flaw.
Bottom right · easy & flatExpected. When nearly everyone gets it right there is little room to separate students.
Bottom left · hard & flatReview first. Students may be guessing, or the wording or a distractor is misleading.
